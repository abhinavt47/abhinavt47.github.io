<h1><strong>Terrific Turtles</strong></h1>
<h2>Coast Cruisers</h2>
<p>Despite being slow on land where they lay their eggs, sea turtles are fast underwater and usually cruise at speeds of 1-6mph, but can sometimes swim up to 20mph in short bursts of speed. A sea turtle's main diet includes fish, crabs, aquatic plants, and even jellyfish. The scales of sea turtles are so tough that they cannot even feel the sting of a jellyfish.</p>
<img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/4767913/pexels-photo-4767913.jpeg" style="width: 600px; height: 500px;">
<h2>River Swimmers</h2>
<p>The usually small freshwater turtles live in shallow waters like ponds, ambushing prey like fish, insects, and foraging for plants. Like their larger sea cousins, they lay their eggs on the shores of the waters they live in, but are usually slower. Sometimes their shells are flexible and rubbery so that the turtles are lighter and more agile underwater.</p>
<img src="https://images.pexels.com/photos/29617401/pexels-photo-29617401.jpeg" style="width: 600px; height: 500px;">
<h2>Armored Tanks</h2>
<p>Tortoises split from the ancestors of modern turtles around 30-50mya. Tortoises are very slow on land due to their low metabolism and heir heavy shells. The shells of tortoises and turtles are not separate from them at all, surprisingly, but are made up of bones like their spine and their ribcage. Their main diet includes leafy greens, grass, hay, and some flowers.</p>
